What Crypto Tax Calculator Does and Why It Matters

Crypto Tax Calculator estimates Turkish crypto tax liability for 2026 from your buy price, sell price, and quantity, applying FIFO or LIFO cost-basis methods and a 15% withholding assumption to estimate your gain/loss and tax owed in TRY.

Cost-basis method matters more than people expect when you've bought the same asset at different prices over time: FIFO (first-in, first-out) and LIFO (last-in, first-out) can produce meaningfully different taxable gain figures for the exact same set of trades, especially in a volatile market. Seeing both methods side by side makes that difference visible instead of hidden inside a single number.

How to Use Crypto Tax Calculator

  1. Enter your buy price, sell price, and quantity for the trade(s) you're estimating.
  2. Choose FIFO or LIFO as the cost-basis method.
  3. Review the estimated gain/loss and tax figure in TRY, based on the 15% withholding assumption.

What to Know

  • Turkish crypto taxation is an evolving area of regulation — the 15% figure used here is a planning assumption, not a confirmed final rule for every scenario, and current regulations should be verified before filing
  • FIFO and LIFO can produce different taxable gain amounts from the same trades; which method actually applies to your situation depends on current regulation and your own trading pattern
  • This covers a straightforward buy/sell estimate — it doesn't model more complex scenarios like staking rewards, airdrops, or crypto-to-crypto trades

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this a confirmed, final tax calculation I can file with?

No — treat it strictly as a planning estimate. Crypto taxation rules in Turkey are still developing; confirm current regulations and your specific liability with a mali müşavir (accountant) before filing anything based on this figure.

Why does the cost-basis method (FIFO vs LIFO) change my result?

If you bought the same asset at different prices over time, which specific purchase is considered sold first changes your calculated gain. In a rising market, FIFO (selling your oldest, usually cheaper, holdings first) typically shows a larger taxable gain than LIFO for the same trade.

Does this cover staking, airdrops, or crypto-to-crypto trades?

No — this covers a straightforward buy/sell scenario. Other crypto activities can have different tax treatment; consult a mali müşavir for anything beyond a simple purchase-and-sale.
