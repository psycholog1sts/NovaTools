What Crypto Price Tracker Does and Why It Matters

Crypto Price Tracker shows current prices for major cryptocurrencies (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, ADA) in TRY, along with 24-hour change, market cap, and volume, with a favorites list for the coins you check most often.

Crypto prices move fast and are usually quoted in USD by default on most trackers — having a quick TRY-denominated view with the key context (change, market cap, volume) in one place saves converting and cross-referencing multiple sources for a fast check.

How to Use Crypto Price Tracker

  1. View current prices and 24-hour change for the listed cryptocurrencies.
  2. Star any coins you want to track as favorites for quick reference.
  3. Check market cap and volume alongside price for additional context.

What to Know

  • Prices reflect a recent market snapshot from the data source, not a continuous live feed tick-by-tick
  • Cryptocurrency prices are highly volatile — a price shown even a few minutes ago may no longer be current
  • This is market data, not investment advice — it doesn't account for your personal financial situation or risk tolerance

Privacy and Security

Viewing prices doesn't require an account, and your favorites list is stored locally in your browser rather than tied to any personal data.

Frequently Asked Questions

How current are the prices shown?

They reflect a recent snapshot from the underlying data source, refreshed periodically rather than streamed continuously. For an actual trade, always check the live price on the exchange you're using at the moment of the transaction.

Should I make investment decisions based on this page?

Use it for a quick reference, not as investment advice. Cryptocurrency is high-risk and highly volatile; any investment decision should be based on your own research and risk tolerance, ideally with professional guidance.

Why might the price here differ slightly from my exchange app?

Different platforms source prices from different exchanges and update on different schedules, so small differences between two legitimate sources at the same moment are normal, not an error.
