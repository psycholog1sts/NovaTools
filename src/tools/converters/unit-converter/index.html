What Unit Converter Does and Why It Matters

Unit Converter converts a value between different units within the same category — length, weight, temperature, volume, and area — using standard, fixed conversion factors (for example, 1 inch = 2.54 centimeters exactly). Enter a value in one unit and the equivalent in other units of the same category is calculated instantly.

This comes up constantly when working across measurement systems: following a recipe that uses a different unit system than you're used to, converting a product's listed dimensions or weight before buying, or checking a measurement from a document, form, or another country's specification. Metric-to-imperial conversion in particular is easy to get wrong by hand because the conversion factors aren't round numbers.

How to Use Unit Converter

  1. Choose the measurement category (length, weight, temperature, volume, or area).
  2. Select the unit you're converting from and enter the value.
  3. Select the unit you're converting to — the result is calculated immediately.
  4. Switch categories or units at any time to run a different conversion.

What's Covered

  • Length: metric (mm, cm, m, km) and imperial (in, ft, yd, mi)
  • Weight/mass: metric (g, kg) and imperial (oz, lb)
  • Temperature: Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin, using their exact conversion formulas rather than a linear approximation
  • Volume and area: standard metric and imperial units for each

Privacy and Security

Values you enter are converted directly in your browser and are never sent anywhere or stored.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the conversion factors exact or approximated?

Standard, internationally defined conversion factors are used (for example, the inch is defined as exactly 2.54 cm). Displayed results may be rounded for readability, but the underlying calculation uses the exact factor, not a rounded shortcut.

Why is temperature conversion different from the others?

Length, weight, and volume conversions are simple multiplication (a fixed ratio between units). Temperature scales have different zero points as well as different scale sizes, so converting between Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin needs a formula with an offset, not just a multiplier.

Which unit system should I use if a recipe or document doesn't specify?

Check the source's country of origin as a hint — US sources typically default to imperial/US customary units, while most of the rest of the world defaults to metric. When genuinely ambiguous, look for a secondary clue like an oven temperature in the document, which often reveals the intended system.
