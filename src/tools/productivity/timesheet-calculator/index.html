The three rules that decide the answer, stated: an end time earlier than its start time is read as a shift that crosses midnight, so 22:00 to 06:00 is eight hours, not minus sixteen; overtime is counted on the weekly total, not per day, so five nine-hour days at a 40-hour threshold give 40 regular and 5 overtime; and rounding is applied to each day separately, to the nearest step you choose, before the week is added up.

What the Timesheet Calculator Does and Why It Matters

Adding up a week of hours by hand is where small errors turn into a wrong paycheque. Seven pairs of times, seven break deductions, a carry across midnight for anyone on nights, and one overtime split — it is not hard arithmetic, but it is exactly the kind of arithmetic people get wrong at the end of a long week. This does it in the browser and shows the working: every day gets its own total, the week total is the sum of those totals, and the regular/overtime split comes from a threshold you can see and change.

It is aimed at the person who needs the number, not at a payroll department: a shift worker checking a paycheque, a freelancer invoicing a client for hours, a small employer working out what to pay before the month closes. Nothing is stored on a server, so the times you type stay on your machine.

How to Use It

  1. Fill in the start and end times for the days you worked. Leave a day blank and it counts as zero.
  2. Put unpaid break time, in minutes, in the break column. It comes off that day only.
  3. Set the overtime threshold to whatever applies to you. Set it to 0 to treat every hour as overtime, or to something higher than your week to treat none of it as overtime.
  4. Add an hourly rate and a multiplier if you want the pay figures. Leave the rate at 0 and the tool just counts hours.
  5. Choose a rounding rule if your employer uses one. "Exact minutes" is the default, because it is the only one that does not change the answer.
  6. Download the CSV to keep it, attach it to an invoice, or paste it into a spreadsheet.

Two Worked Examples

A day shift with a lunch break. 09:00 to 17:30 with a 30-minute unpaid break is 8 hours 30 minutes of clock time minus 30 minutes, so the day total is 8:00. Five of those is 40:00 for the week, which at a 40-hour threshold is all regular and no overtime.

A night shift across midnight. 22:00 to 06:00 with no break is 8:00. The end time is earlier than the start time, so the tool reads it as the next morning rather than as a negative day. If you actually meant a shift that ended before it began, that is not a shift, and no tool can tell the difference — check the column before you trust the total.

What to Know

  • Overtime here is weekly, not daily, and not jurisdictional. Some places pay overtime after 8 hours in a day, some after 40 in a week, some both, some at different multipliers for different bands. This tool implements one rule: everything over the weekly threshold, at one multiplier. If your rule is different, this will give you the wrong number, and it will give it to you confidently.
  • It is not a legal or payroll authority. It does not know your jurisdiction's minimum wage, rest-break law, holiday pay, night premiums, or tax. It multiplies hours by a rate you typed.
  • No timezone or daylight-saving handling. Times are read as clock times on a single day. A week containing a DST change will be off by an hour on that day, and you should correct that day by hand.
  • A break longer than the shift is refused, not carried as negative time: the day shows 0:00 and the status line says which day was rejected.
  • Rounding is applied per day, before the week is summed. Rounding the week instead would give a different answer; if your employer rounds the weekly total rather than each day, this is not the tool for that.
  • Nothing is stored between visits, and nothing is uploaded. Closing the tab discards the week — download the CSV first.

Privacy and Security

Every calculation runs in your browser. The times, the rate and the totals are never sent to NovaTools or anywhere else, and the CSV is built in the page and handed straight to your downloads folder. There is no account, no upload step, and nothing kept after you close the tab.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is overtime worked out per day or per week?

Per week. The daily totals are added up first, then everything above the threshold you set is overtime. Five nine-hour days at a 40-hour threshold give 40 regular hours and 5 overtime hours, not 5 hours of daily overtime.

How does it handle a shift that ends after midnight?

An end time earlier than the start time is read as the next morning, so 22:00 to 06:00 is 8:00. That is an assumption, and it is the only reasonable one a calculator can make from two clock times — but it does mean a genuinely mistyped pair of times will produce a plausible-looking number.

Why is my total different from my employer's?

Almost always rounding or the overtime rule. Employers commonly round each punch to the nearest 6 or 15 minutes, and some count overtime daily. Set the rounding rule to match theirs and check whether their overtime is daily; if it is, this tool cannot reproduce it.

Does it convert between hours and decimal?

Yes — the "Show hours as" control switches every figure on the page and in the CSV between 8:30 and 8.50. Payroll systems usually want decimal.

Can I save my week?

Not on this site. Download the CSV — it carries every day, both totals and the settings that produced them, so the week can be reconstructed or checked later.
